# Monetization — Mycroft

**Pricing hypothesis:** one fixed-price diagnostic engagement at **€3,500**, ten working
days, invoiced on signature and due in 14 days, with no hourly component anywhere in the
offer. Every number in this file is a hypothesis being tested, not a forecast.

**Break-even:** the lane covers its own cost at **two engagements per half-year**. It does
not pay a salary, and it is not meant to. Working backwards: €3,500 per engagement, minus
payment fees (~€40), minus the founder's time (10 days), gives an effective day rate well
below a normal consultancy rate. That is intentional and it is the trade being made —
bounded, ten days, then out — for a method that is repeatable by someone else. If the
founder wants a day rate that matches a management consultancy, the engagement has to be
sold at €12,000+ and will find a much smaller market.

**Kill trigger:** kill criterion **K1** fires at week 8 — zero paid engagements after three
qualified conversations means the lane stops, and it stops by *not lowering the price*.
Discounting converts "the price is wrong" and "we are desperate" into one indistinguishable
signal, and the second one destroys the positioning this lane is built on. The second
trigger is **K6** at month 12: if the method cannot be delivered by a second person, the
honest description becomes a practice, the price is re-derived from that, and the "product"
framing is dropped from all public material.

## What is being sold, precisely

A written document, ten working days, containing the six sections in `README.md`. The
client's decision at the end is: *which of our AI ideas do we fund, and which do we kill.*

That decision has a value. A mid-sized company's board has usually already spent €50k–€200k
committing to the wrong one of these ideas. Knowing which idea to stop, ten days from now,
is worth more than the invoice.

## The price ladder

| Step | Offer | Price | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 90-minute scoping call | free | Qualify. Also the primary discovery artefact — most "we need a CAIO" conversations are answered in the call. |
| 2 | **Diagnostic engagement** | €3,500 fixed | The product. Ten working days, six sections, kill list. |
| 3 | Diagnostic + 90-day check-in | €4,900 fixed | Closes the loop the client will otherwise forget. |
| 4 | Two-day group workshop | €2,400 per seat, min 4 | The method taught, not performed. Finds future buyers. |

Steps 3 and 4 do not exist yet. They are written down so that the *shape* of the ladder is
visible now, and both are gated on K1 not firing.

## Where the price came from, and what would move it

€3,500 is anchored on the client's avoided cost, not on the founder's day rate:

- One avoided quarter of engineering on the wrong pilot: easily €30k–€60k.
- One regulatory finding that would have surfaced six months later: unbounded.
- One board meeting that goes badly because the answer is not ready: unquantifiable, and
  probably the real reason people buy.

Upward pressure on price if: the kill list is being quoted back to other clients (proven
value), or three engagements in a row come from referrals (demand exceeds capacity).
Downward pressure if: conversations stall on price rather than on scope or credibility.
That distinction is the whole reason `docs/LOG.md` records *which* of the three it was.

## Deliberately absent

- No revenue dependent on token prices, mining emissions, or crypto appreciation. That is
  what killed `decentralized-ai-mining` as a business rather than a research question.
- No "passive income" framing. Every euro here is attached to ten days of a person's
  attention, and the document says so.
- No revenue from the diagnostic method being published or open-sourced. If the method goes
  public, the engagements become worthless and the lane dies — so the method stays
  private, and `221b` documents the thesis without the instrument.

## The uncomfortable arithmetic

At three engagements per half-year, this lane is roughly €21k/year gross. That is a
side income, not a company, and it is what a solo operator with 10–12 hours a week can
actually sustain alongside `hansom-cab`.

The realistic ceiling, if the method transfers and referrals take over, is 8–10 engagements
a year — roughly €30k–€40k, with a client base that makes the founder the obvious first
call for a Belgian company facing an AI obligation. That is the whole prize, it is worth
having, and it is three orders of magnitude smaller than the six-repo plan ori originally
proposed. The plan should be reported at that scale or not at all.

## Evidence that would change these numbers

- Kill trigger K1 firing (three conversations, zero sales) → the price is irrelevant; the
  message or the market is wrong.
- Two or more engagements bought as implementation (K2) → the offer is being read as a
  build contract; the exclusions and the "we do not build it" clause need to move into the
  first paragraph.
- A delivered diagnostic that produces no kill-list action → the deliverable is not the
  thing being valued, and the offer should be restructured around a different output.
